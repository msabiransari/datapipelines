Copying puts the text on your clipboard and nothing else: it sends no request and grants no access. Your agent works with the permissions of its own API key. Your role here does not author dashboards, so copy the request for a collaborator who can.

Dashboards bring pipeline results, visualizations and controls together in one view. They help people explore a question and compare outcomes without rebuilding the underlying analysis. For example, combine trip volume and market-share charts with borough and period filters.

  1. Describe who the dashboard is for, the question it answers, its sources, and the charts and filters you want.
  2. Ask a connected agent to build the dashboard as a draft, validate it and test the visualizations it writes.
  3. Open the draft it returns, refresh its board to check the live data, and review its sources and visualizations. Someone allowed to release dashboards releases it from its workspace when it is ready.
  • Pipelines are a dashboard's sources: it pins exact pipeline versions and shows their results.
  • Visualizations render those results as charts, tables or KPIs on the board.
  • Parameter Sets can supply the board's filters and controls.

A common chain is pipelines, then visualizations, then dashboards; not every dashboard needs every link.
