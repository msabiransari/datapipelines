What datasources are for

Datasources are the registered database connections everything else reads through — pipelines, dashboards, templates' data-backed choices. A datasource is registered once with its connection facts and credential, tested, and then granted to the workspaces that need it. What this page counts is what your workspace can see.

Explore a datasource with your agent

  1. Register the datasource here in the workspace if it does not exist yet — a person does that, because registering means handing over a credential. Test the connection from its page.
  2. Ask a connected agent to explore what the datasource holds: its schemas, tables and columns, a few preview rows, and whether a bounded read-only query answers your question better.
  3. Read the report it returns and build on what it verified. The agent reads; changes to the datasource itself stay in the workspace's hands.

Copying puts the text on your clipboard and nothing else: it sends no request and grants no access. Your agent works with the permissions of its own API key. Your role here does not read row data, so copy the request for a collaborator who can.

Related resources and help

  • Pipelines read through a datasource's connection when they run.
  • Templates offer choices that come from a datasource's data.
  • Dashboards refresh through the pipelines those datasources feed.

Registering or editing a datasource is done by a person with the workspace's credentials — no agent or tool registers one.
